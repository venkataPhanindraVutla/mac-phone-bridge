# Milestone 0.4 results

Branch `milestone-0.4`. Like the 0.3 file, this has two parts: checks that were run without a phone (with measured results), and checks the owner must run on a real phone and Mac (each with steps and a `PENDING` result line to fill in). It then lists the copy the implementers wrote for the owner to review, the decisions made during the build, and the known limitations.

Milestone 0.4 covers control and context: a media controller (Now Playing on the Mac, media keys, sliders in the Phone window), Do Not Disturb and Focus sync, auto-lock when you walk away (a Bluetooth beacon from the phone), and the phone as a trackpad and presentation remote.

Nothing in Part 2 has been run. Every claim about how macOS or Android behaves on a device (media key routing, Focus status in an ad-hoc build, Android 15+ modes, Bluetooth delivery to a menu-bar app, a real lock) is **unverified** until the owner fills in its result line.

## Technical terms used in this document

- **BLE (Bluetooth Low Energy):** the low-power radio mode used for the walk-away beacon. The phone only *advertises* (broadcasts a small packet); the Mac only *scans*. Nothing connects.
- **RSSI:** received signal strength, in dBm. Closer to zero is stronger (−50 is near, −85 is far). It is noisy: a pocket or a body can cost 10 dBm or more.
- **Epoch:** the 15-minute period (`floor(unix time / 900)`) that the beacon's ID is computed for. The ID changes each epoch so the phone can't be tracked; the Mac accepts the previous, current and next epoch.
- **TCC:** macOS's privacy system ("Transparency, Consent and Control"). It owns the Bluetooth, Accessibility and Focus status prompts and grants.
- **Focus:** macOS's Do Not Disturb family. There is no public API for an app to switch one on, so the Mac app runs Shortcuts.
- **Implicit zen rule ("mode"):** on Android 15+, an app that changes Do Not Disturb does not change the system-wide setting; it turns its own automatic rule on and off. Settings shows it as a "mode" named after the app.
- **CGEvent:** a macOS low-level input event. The trackpad feature posts these to move the pointer, click and scroll; that needs the Accessibility grant.
- **Ad-hoc signed:** a build signed without an Apple developer team (the Contributor build). macOS treats each new build as a new app.
- **Contributor build / Full build / oss / loopback:** as in the 0.3 file.

### (e) Auto-lock

Auto-lock never unlocks the Mac. It locks only after the Mac has been idle (see the delay below), only after the phone was first seen near, and only while the phone has had a Wi-Fi session with the Mac in the last 30 minutes (R32). It is deliberately slow and conservative (R33).

1. **Turn it on.** Turn on "Let your Mac lock when you walk away" in the phone app (it asks for Nearby devices on Android 12+; on Android 14+ also check the service starts with the permission granted, and fails cleanly with it refused: a clean failure means the phone's row says "Android Mirroring needs the Nearby devices permission to do this. Allow it in Settings." and the app does not crash or stop its connection). Turn on "Lock this Mac when <phone> is away" in the Mac Settings. **The Mac shows the macOS Bluetooth prompt only now:** record whether it appeared, what its text says, and what the Settings line says if you refuse it ("Allow Bluetooth for Android Mirroring in System Settings.") and if Bluetooth is off ("Turn on Bluetooth on this Mac.").
2. **Test mode and the threshold.** Turn on "Test mode" (it shows readouts and never locks). Read the "Signal: n dBm" value with the phone at the desk in front of you (a minute each), in a trouser pocket on your body, in a bag, and in the next room with the door closed. Write down the median of each. Choose a "Signal threshold" that is below the first two and above the last two. The default is −80 dBm; the risk to check is a pocket reading that hovers around −80.
3. **Walk away.** Leave the Mac and walk out of the room with the phone. At the defaults a normal walk-away locks after about 60 s or more (the Mac must have been idle for at least max(delay, 60 s), and the phone must read as away for the delay); the upper bound is about 90 s. Nothing may touch the keyboard or mouse meanwhile. Time it. It stays locked and does not unlock when you come back. If the phone is turned off or its app is killed at close range, the Mac waits longer (about 2.5 minutes) before locking, on purpose; time that too.

## Decisions made during the build

### The plan's ten header deviations from the design notes

Each: what, why, and the cost if wrong.

1. **The BLE ID includes the phone's fingerprint.** The discovery key is per Mac, so two phones paired with the same Mac would have got the same ID. Cost if wrong: none visible; IDs stay 8 bytes.
2. **The Mac scans without a service filter and matches the service data itself.** The legacy 31-byte advertisement leaves no room for a 128-bit service UUID next to the data, so a service-UUID filter cannot match it. Cost if wrong: the Mac handles more advertisements than it needs.
3. **The sliders are in a Now Playing tab of the Phone window, not in the menu.** A menu-style MenuBarExtra cannot show sliders. Cost if wrong: one more click to seek.
4. **`DndAvailability{sends, applies}` plus `DndState.snapshot`.** A side can send changes without applying the other's, and a snapshot of the current state at connect must never be applied. Cost if wrong: a change in the first second after connect could be applied wrongly.
5. **Availability reasons UNAVAILABLE=4 and NEEDS_SETUP=5.** The existing reasons could not say "can't advertise" or "shortcuts missing". Cost if wrong: one wire-enum change (done early in Task 1).
6. **The 5 s button release counts from the last `input.*` frame.** A held button must not stay down if the phone goes quiet. Cost if wrong: a very long, perfectly still hold is released after 5 s.
7. **Auto-lock arms only after the phone was first seen near, and the phone's availability is stored per phone.** Otherwise enabling it with the phone out of range would lock at once. Cost if wrong: it does not lock until you were near once.
8. **The phone advertises for one Mac only.** Amended by R31 below, which advertises for every paired Mac (up to three). Cost if wrong: a phone advertising for several Macs uses slightly more power; see R31.
9. **Test mode ignores the idle time.** Test mode shows "Would lock now" and never locks, so idle is not a safety question there. Cost if wrong: test mode shows a lock that real use would delay.
10. **Copy: the design's "Check again" button is written "Check Again"** (macOS title case). Cost if wrong: none.

### Controller rulings made while the build ran

- **R25: After Task 1, the Mac and Android lanes ran in parallel in separate worktrees (Android 2→4→6→8, Mac 3→5→7→9), each reviewed in its lane, then merged; interop case 9 and this task came after.** The owner asked for parallel builds where possible. The lanes touch disjoint files. Cost if wrong: one merge conflict. (The merge needed one doc fix, R28's correction of the SPEC §21 example, and no code conflict.)
- **R26: The artwork-key test vectors use short inputs at block boundaries (empty, "jpeg", and deterministic 55, 56, 64 and 65 bytes), not a 96 KB input.** The key is a SHA-256 prefix, so a 96 KB input tests nothing more and bloats every vector diff. The 96 KB cap is an encoder test in Task 2. Cost if wrong: none.
- **R27: Mac media commands are sent unless the phone has said media is off (no report counts as allowed); a phone-reported "off" also clears Now Playing.** Commands are user-triggered (R22 covers only automatic streams) and the phone gates them itself, and a frozen, still-ticking track after switch-off would mislead. Cost if wrong: a command right after connect could be refused by the phone.
- **R28: SPEC §21's rule wins over its example for the DND echo guard.** Any local transition clears the last-applied match; a user's off-then-on after an apply goes out as USER; only an unbroken late match goes as SYNC and is never applied. Following the example would leave the two sides out of step. Cost if wrong: none (the SPEC example was fixed in `7eaa7fa`).
- **R29: The beacon rotation is scheduled with `AlarmManager` RTC_WAKEUP (an inexact window of 5 minutes, or `setAndAllowWhileIdle` when idle), and the epoch is recomputed on each alarm and on time change or screen on; no exact-alarm permission.** A coroutine delay stalls in CPU deep sleep, leaving a stale ID, so the Mac would see the phone as absent and lock. The Mac's ±1 epoch slack covers inexact alarms, and exact alarms need a user grant on Android 14+. Cost if wrong: a rotation more than 15 minutes late under extreme Doze, and the Mac sees the phone absent (owner check (e)8).
- **R30: One legacy advertising set per Mac, rotated by changing its data (the slot is never released); the beacon states are IDLE, STARTING, ON and FAILED; a confirmed failure reports `proximity.availability` UNAVAILABLE at once, and retries run at 5, 15, 60 s, then every 5 minutes.** An armed Mac must learn that the phone stopped advertising. Cost if wrong: none.
- **R31 (amends deviation 8): the phone advertises for every paired Mac, up to 3 sets, in priority order connected first, then by last connection.** A phone connected to Mac A must not look absent to Mac B, where it was last seen near. Cost if wrong: slightly more advertising (low power, non-connectable). No set is preempted for a lower-priority one (fail-safe: preempting would silence an unconnected Mac).
- **R32: The Mac's auto-lock additionally requires a Wi-Fi session with that phone now or within the last 30 minutes (stored per phone), and an availability that goes back to enabled needs a new "near" sighting before re-arming.** The Mac cannot learn that the phone stopped advertising while there is no session (the switch or Bluetooth turned off, the process killed); this bounds that window without breaking walk-away (the session is usually up or just dropped). Cost if wrong: no auto-lock if the phone has not reached this Mac over Wi-Fi for 30 minutes.
- **R33: Auto-lock is stricter than the brief's far rule.** A "scanner stalled" blocker (nothing heard from any device for 20 s; the scan is re-issued and an App Nap activity is held while on). Absence that begins from at least 5 dB above the threshold counts as far only after max(4×delay, 120 s), then the delay runs. A "Mac in use" blocker while a display-sleep-preventing assertion is held (a video, a call, a browser wake lock). Idle of at least max(delay, 60 s). A present-but-weak signal needs max(2×delay, 60 s). Each is a realistic false lock in 8 hours, and a missed lock costs far less. Cost if wrong: auto-lock is slower (about 60 s after walking away, about 2.5 minutes after an abrupt vanish), and does not lock at all while a wake lock is held.
- **R34: The laser panel's collection behaviour adds `.fullScreenAuxiliary` (level `.screenSaver`).** The plan's behaviour set hides the dot over a full-screen Keynote or PowerPoint slideshow, which is the main use of a presentation remote. Cost if wrong: none. Whether it shows when "Allow Mission Control" is off is owner check (f)7.

### Other behaviour for the results (from the reviews)

- **Android below 15 and 15+ differ for DND.** Below 15 (or an app targeting below 35) the phone's sync changes the global DND directly, so Mac "off" clears a DND the user turned on by hand. On 15+ it only turns the app's own mode on and off, so Mac "off" leaves a hand-set DND on and the sides then differ until the user changes one (accepted; documented in the code).
- **Simultaneous opposite changes end stable but disagreeing** (phone on, Mac off in the same second): neither is re-sent, so they stay different until the next change on either side.
- **Unconnected paired Macs cannot be told about a phone-side fault** (beacon failed, Bluetooth switched off) because there is no session; the 30-minute rule (R32) bounds it.

## Known limitations in 0.4

- **Auto-lock is conservative by design (R33):**
  - It never locks while a video, a call or a browser wake lock keeps the screen awake (any page holding a wake lock, `caffeinate -d`, Amphetamine and similar tools block it indefinitely; Settings shows the line).
  - It never locks in a room where the phone is the only Bluetooth advertiser (the scanner-stalled check needs another device in the air to tell a quiet room from a dead scanner). A partial stall, where other devices keep changing their addresses, can still look like a walk-away.
  - After an abrupt vanish (the phone turned off or its app killed at close range) it waits up to about 2.5 minutes. After a normal walk-away it needs about 60 s.
  - It locks only after the Mac has been idle for max(delay, 60 s), and a weak signal at the desk needs max(2×delay, 60 s) of no input.
- **A Mac the phone has not reached over Wi-Fi for 30 minutes does not auto-lock (R32).** If the app quits while connected, the Mac may stay paused until the phone reconnects.
- Auto-lock follows one phone and never unlocks (PRD F-15). The phone advertises for up to three paired Macs (R31), not more; the connected Mac has priority and a lower-priority Mac is not preempted.
- Unconnected paired Macs cannot learn that the phone's beacon failed (no session): the 30-minute rule is the bound.
- The beacon's rotation under extreme Doze can slip by many minutes (`setAndAllowWhileIdle` fires at most about every 9 minutes). Past ±15 minutes the Mac sees the phone as absent (R29).
- Android's own Bluetooth address rotation is not aligned with the 15-minute epochs.
- The default −80 dBm with a phone in a pocket is borderline; use test mode to pick a threshold (owner check (e)2).
- The lock call is a private macOS function loaded at run time. Its return code is not documented and is only logged. It is untested on a real lock until the owner checks it.
- **macOS has no public Focus API.** Phone to Mac DND needs the two Shortcuts the user makes once. Any Mac Focus counts as "on". Rename a Shortcut and the sync stops until "Check Again". A run is cut off after 10 s, which stops the command-line tool but may not stop a Shortcut already handed to the Shortcuts app.
- On Android 15+, the phone shows an app "mode", and turning Focus off on the Mac may leave a DND the user turned on by hand still on. Below 15 it clears it.
- Focus status may be unreadable in an ad-hoc build (check (d)); then Mac to phone is off and only phone to Mac works.
- **Media keys may not route to an app that plays no audio.** macOS may not send them to a menu-bar app; the menu and Phone window tab controls always work. Not verified (check (b)).
- The seek and volume sliders are in the Phone window's Now Playing tab, not in the menu.
- A media session with a live stream has no seek. A track's artwork over 96 KB after four compression steps is not sent. The Mac keeps the 8 most recent artworks in memory only and clears them on disconnect.
- **Every ad-hoc rebuild loses the Accessibility grant** (the code identity changes each time, so macOS treats the app as new). A team-signed build keeps it.
- While the phone streams pointer moves, a physical mouse moved at the same time is ignored until the phone pauses for about 100 ms (check (f)5).
- The laser dot is drawn on the screen under the pointer only. Whether it shows with Keynote's "Allow app switching while presenting" off is not verified.
- The trackpad surface is not usable with TalkBack (it takes explore-by-touch); the Remote tab is. The laser pad has no accessibility description.
- The home row "Use as trackpad" is hidden for a Mac without `input.v1`.
- The Mac's Settings may show a blank "Mac to phone" Focus line until the phone first reports (check (c)8).
- When the Mac disconnects while the Trackpad screen is open, the screen closes and shows no reason (check (f)10).
- Whether the Mac tracks Wi-Fi sessions of a phone that doesn't offer auto-lock is untested (check (e)12).
- The "phone is nearby, try connecting" hint (Architecture §3.1) is not built.
- Not exercised without hardware: everything in Part 2 above, including a real lock, Now Playing and the media keys, Focus changes by a Shortcut, the Bluetooth, Accessibility and Focus status prompts, and the phone's Bluetooth advertising.
