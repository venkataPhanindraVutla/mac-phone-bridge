# Milestone 0.5 results

Branch `milestone-0.5`. Like the 0.4 file, this has two parts: checks that were run without a phone (with measured results), and checks the owner must run on a real phone and Mac (each with steps, a pass line and a `PENDING` result line to fill in). It then lists the copy the implementers wrote for the owner to review, the decisions made during the build, and the known limitations.

Milestone 0.5 is Full mode: the Mac shows the phone's screen live in a window, plays the phone's audio, and the Mac's mouse, trackpad and keyboard control the phone. It needs a one-time setup on the phone with Wireless debugging. The phone app pairs with Wireless debugging, starts a small helper (scrcpy's server, with two patches) as the `shell` user, and copies the helper's video, audio and control bytes to the Mac over three new encrypted links. The Mac decodes and plays them and sends the input back.

Nothing in Part 2 has been run. Every claim about how a real phone or Mac behaves (pairing on a real phone, the helper starting, delay and frame rate, typing with an input method, audio, clipboard, screen off and back on, battery) is **unverified** until the owner fills in its result line. The keep-alive patch that was merged into this branch (liveness pings, background-power guidance) has its own owner checks, still pending, in [the keep-alive results](0.1-keepalive-results.md).

## Technical terms used in this document

- **scrcpy and the helper:** scrcpy is an open-source program that shows an Android phone's screen on a computer and sends input back. Its phone-side part is a small Java server. The "helper" is that server, built from its source (version 5.0.1, with two patches) and shipped inside the phone app. It runs only while the Mac shows the screen.
- **ADB, Wireless debugging, pairing code, pairing port and connect port:** ADB is Android's developer connection; a computer connected by it can start programs with the rights of the `shell` user. Wireless debugging (in Developer options) offers it over Wi-Fi. To trust a new device, the phone shows "Pair device with pairing code": a six-digit pairing code and a **pairing port**. After pairing, ADB connects on a different **connect port**, shown on the main Wireless debugging screen. Mixing the two ports up is the commonest mistake, so the app says which one it got.
- **NSD / mDNS:** Android's name discovery (NsdManager), which uses multicast DNS. The phone app uses it to find its own pairing and connect ports without you typing them.
- **Abstract socket:** a local socket on the phone with a name instead of a file. The helper listens on one (`scrcpy_<8 hex digits>`); only programs on the same phone can open it, and patch P1 lets in only the `shell` user.
- **Bulk link and relay:** a bulk link is one of the extra encrypted connections between phone and Mac (from 0.3) used for large data. Mirroring uses three: video, audio and control. The relay is the phone app's loop that copies bytes between the helper's local sockets and these links without reading them.
- **H.264 / H.265, key frame, SPS/PPS, PTS:** H.264 and H.265 are video formats (H.265 is smaller for the same quality but not every phone can produce it). A key frame is a full picture; the frames between depend on it, so a viewer that joins late or drops data must wait for the next one. SPS and PPS are the small setup records at the start of the stream that a decoder needs. PTS is each frame's timestamp, in microseconds.
- **VideoToolbox:** Apple's hardware video decoder, which the Mac app uses to decode the phone's video.
- **Opus / AAC and the jitter buffer:** Opus and AAC are audio formats (Opus is the default; AAC is the fallback). The jitter buffer holds a short amount of audio (about 60 ms) so that uneven arrival over Wi-Fi does not make the sound crackle.
- **Input method and marked text:** the macOS way of typing Japanese, Chinese and similar text: you type sounds, the system shows underlined "marked text" you are still composing, and a candidate window offers choices until you commit one.
- **Lateness, preset, round trip:** lateness is how far behind real time the picture is when it is decoded. A preset is a quality setting (High, Balanced or Low; each sets resolution, bit rate and frame rate), and Auto picks one by watching lateness. A round trip is a tap going to the phone and the changed picture coming back, measured on the Mac to the moment it is decoded.
- **LGPL:** the Lesser GPL, a library licence. A program under another licence may ship an LGPL library if the library stays unmodified and replaceable and its licence and source link are included.
- **Contributor build / Full build / oss / loopback:** as in the 0.3 file.

### (b) Delay and frame rate (exit criterion)

Target (the exit criterion): on 5 GHz Wi-Fi, the median round trip at High is **under 80 ms** and the frame rate is **55 fps or more**. The delay is a round trip measured to the Mac's decode, not to the screen (see the known limitations).

1. Put the Mac and the phone on the same 5 GHz Wi-Fi. On the Mac, open Terminal and run `ping -c 100 <phone ip>` (the phone's IP is in its Wi-Fi settings). Write down the average time and the loss as the baseline.
2. In the Mac menu choose "Show Phone Screen". Wait for the picture. Keep the phone screen on, on the home screen.
3. In the mirror window, hold the Option key and click the Quality button in the toolbar: choose "Measure Delay…". Do not touch anything for about 10 seconds. The phone shows a black test screen with four white squares in its corners, the Mac taps its centre, and then an alert titled "Delay" appears with the round-trip median and 95th percentile, the number of taps (and how many were missed), and the frame rate.
4. Do this 3 times at **High**, 3 times at **Balanced** and 3 times at **Low** (Quality menu, choose the preset by hand first). Record the median, the 95th percentile (p95), the misses and the fps from the alert, and from the `delay median … p95 …` line in the Mac log.
5. If the alert says "The test screen didn't open on the phone.", the phone took longer than 5 seconds to open the screen or the screen was not shown. Record that and try again. No tap is sent in that case.
6. If High's median is between 80 and 120 ms, film both screens at 240 fps: put the Mac window beside the phone, start a stopwatch app on the phone, film both, and count the frames between the phone's time and the Mac's copy of it. Divide by 240 and take half for the one-way delay.
7. Repeat steps 3 and 4 once with the Mac next to the router.
8. Look at the four white squares on the test screen: are they whole, or cut off by the phone's rounded corners or camera cutout, and do they survive at Low?

**Pass:** at High, median under 80 ms and fps 55 or more, on at least the reference phone over 5 GHz.

## Decisions made during the build

### The plan's eleven header deviations from the design notes

Each: what, why, and the cost if wrong. **Deviations 1 and 2 need the owner's decision.**

1. **Needs the owner's decision. The phone's clipboard reaches the Mac automatically only while the phone screen is shown.** The PRD and roadmap say "automatic in Full mode". The clipboard comes from the helper, which runs only during a mirror session; a helper and an ADB link kept running all day would cost battery against the PRD's own under-2%-a-day goal and widen the attack surface (the helper running after the user turned Full mode off). Outside a session the 0.3 tile, notification action and Share sheet still work. Cost if wrong: a 0.9 change to keep a lightweight helper running (ruling R38).
2. **Needs the owner's decision. `spake2-android` is LGPL-3.0, with prebuilt libraries.** libadb-android needs it for pairing. The default is to ship it unmodified, with its licence and a source link, and keep the project Apache-2.0 (ruling R39). Cost if wrong: F-Droid needs it built from source (a recorded follow-up with the JitPack item), or a Kotlin SPAKE2 is written later.
3. **Two helper patches (P1 peer check, P2 sensitive clips).** The socket name carries only 31 random bits, so any app could race the connect; a password copied on the phone would otherwise reach the Mac pasteboard. Cost if wrong: patches to carry on each upstream update; the helper version string is `5.0.1-am1`.
4. **`MirrorStart` also carries `session` and `port`; `MirrorState` and `MirrorStop` carry `session`.** A late STOPPED from an old session must not close a new one, and the bulk port changes after wake. Cost if wrong: two more fields.
5. **`mirror.state` has no width, height or codec.** The relay does not parse the stream, and the stream's own session packets give the size. Cost if wrong: none, the Mac reads the stream.
6. **`mirror.start` has no `screen_off`.** The Mac sends the display-power message on the helper's control link itself. Cost if wrong: none; the helper's own cleanup restores the display on any other ending.
7. **The test pattern starts with `am start` over the phone's own ADB link.** Android 10+ forbids an app from opening an activity from the background. Cost if wrong: one more message type.
8. **The ADB link stays open between sessions** (until the Mac disconnects, or 10 minutes after the last session), and the last connect port is cached. Otherwise every adaptive-quality restart repeats NSD discovery. Cost if wrong: an open link on the phone for 10 minutes.
9. **Clipboard autosync follows the phone's "Share clipboard" switch,** set at launch, and the Mac drops helper text while the last availability is off. Cost if wrong: none visible.
10. **Every special key is a key-down and key-up per Mac `keyDown`, repeats included.** Nothing is ever held on the phone, so losing focus cannot strand a key. Command-Backspace is Shift+Move Home then Delete. Cost if wrong: a held key repeats at the Mac's rate.
11. **Copy uses macOS title case** ("Show Phone Screen", "Measure Delay…"). Cost if wrong: none.

### Controller rulings made while the build ran

- **R38: phone-to-Mac clipboard autosync runs only while a mirror session runs.** (Deviation 1.) The always-on alternative breaks the battery goal and widens the attack surface. Cost if wrong: a 0.9 change.
- **R39: ship `spake2-android` 2.2.1 (LGPL-3.0) unmodified, separately replaceable, with its licence text and a source link; the project stays Apache-2.0.** (Deviation 2.) Cost if wrong: an F-Droid from-source build, or a Kotlin SPAKE2.
- **R40: after Task 1, the Android lane (2→3→4) and Mac lane (5→6→7→8→9) ran in separate worktrees.** Task 2's fixture corrections were to be merged into the Mac lane before Task 6. Cost if wrong: a merge conflict.
- **R41: licence texts ship inside the APK (`assets/licenses/`) and on each release; an in-app licences screen waits for 0.9.** LGPL-3.0 §4 needs the licence texts to accompany the binary. Cost if wrong: a later screen, no relicensing.
- **R42: lanes were re-cut from `milestone-0.5` instead of merging lane a5 into m5 (R40's merge).** The worktrees had been removed and both lanes needed the keep-alive changes. Cost: none, same content.
- **R43: libadb's `AdbMdns` port probe is acceptable only if it binds and closes within the call and is never `accept()`ed.** (It held; R47 then removed it.) Cost: none.
- **R44: horizontal scroll sends `h = −scrollingDeltaX / 24`, the sign flipped from the brief.** Mac +X is toward the left edge; Android +HSCROLL scrolls right; scrcpy's own client flips it too. Cost: one line.
- **R45: the touch-up sent at session end is delivered: closing the session drains queued control bytes for up to 200 ms first.** A lost UP leaves the phone with a stuck touch. Cost: up to 200 ms slower stop.
- **R46: pairing failure stages are owned, not inferred from stack frames.** WRONG_PORT when the port equals a known connect port or a failure follows a successful connect; WRONG_CODE for libadb's post-handshake failures; the frame check stays only as a hint. Cost: more tests.
- **R47: libadb's `AdbMdns` is replaced by our own `NsdManager` finder.** The library's one leaks a discovery stopped before it starts and never retries a resolve. Cost: one small class.
- **R48: the pairing port is not pre-probed (amends R46); only the connect port is.** adbd's pairing server is single-use, and a connect-and-close probe likely burns the code. Cost: NOT_LISTENING for pairing comes from libadb's own refused connect.
- **R49: a timed-out pairing attempt may leave one libadb thread until the peer closes.** The mutex and screen are freed at once; reaching libadb's socket would break the no-listener rule. Cost: a parked thread per timed-out attempt (watched in check (a) step 13).
- **R50: with notifications blocked or denied, the Full mode screen shows in-app "Pairing port" and code fields.** Without them pairing is a dead end on Android 13+ with notifications denied. Cost: two fields; one extra tap after a permanent refusal.
- **R51: audio keeps the plan's 60/150/40 ms jitter policy; the "pull latency back between 60 and 150 ms" idea is not adopted.** Revisit with measured latency. Cost: up to 150 ms of audio lag in drift cases.
- **R52: `DelayTestActivity` is `exported="true"` with `android:permission="android.permission.DUMP"` (amends the global constraint).** The shell must start it with `am start`; DUMP is held by `shell` and cannot be granted to apps. Cost: one manifest attribute; revisit if Play review objects.
- **R53: the end-of-session kill matches the helper's own command line (`com.genymobile.scrcpy.Server 5.0.1-am1`), not the jar path.** CLASSPATH is an environment variable, so the path never appears on the command line. Cost: one constant. (Since the final review wave each session pushes its own jar, `/data/local/tmp/anmirror-helper-<scid>.jar`, so an earlier session's clean-up, which unlinks the jar in its CLASSPATH as it starts, can no longer delete the next session's; the kill pattern is unchanged.)
- **R54: no libadb fork; the helper-to-Mac relay is bounded by a watchdog.** If the video backlog passes 4 MB, or a single write to the Mac blocks for over 2 s (audio: 512 KB or 2 s), the session ends FAILED with reason LINK_SLOW (a new value, 9, in `mirror.proto` and SPEC §24). The Mac shows "The connection was too slow to show the phone screen." Adaptive quality steps down before this trips in normal use. Cost: a hard stop instead of graceful degradation on a stalled link.
- **R55: echo suppression.** Everything the Mac gives the phone is remembered for 5 s (at most 64 items, cleared on disconnect) and ignored once when the helper reports it back. Typed non-Latin text, which is sent as a paste per keystroke, otherwise lands on the Mac's clipboard. Cost: a deliberate same-text phone copy within 5 s is ignored once.
- **R56: the Measure Delay test screen carries a marker.** The phone's `DelayTestActivity` draws four white corner squares on black; the Mac taps only after seeing the marker on 3 consecutive frames and at least 500 ms after sending DELAY. If it is not seen within 5 s, no tap is ever sent and the alert says "The test screen didn't open on the phone." A dark user screen must never receive a tap. Cost: one drawing change and a frame check.
- **R57 (final review): the LINK_SLOW video limit is about 3 s at the session's bit rate,** `video_bit_rate / 8 × 3` bytes, kept between 512 KiB and 4 MiB (amends R54's flat 4 MiB, which let the picture trail some 17 s at Low). Audio keeps 512 KiB and the 2 s write stall. Cost: none; Auto still acts first.
- **R58 (final review): every ADB connect proves its peer is the phone's own adbd.** Over the new connection the app runs `am broadcast -a io.github.anmirror.phone.ADB_PROOF -n io.github.anmirror.phone/.full.AdbProofReceiver --es n <128-bit nonce>`; the receiver is exported behind DUMP (like R52), so only the shell can deliver it. No matching broadcast within 3 s: the connection is closed, the cached port forgotten, `full-mode adb-proof-failed` logged, and Full mode needs re-enabling (NEEDS_SETUP). No certificate pinning, because adbd's certificate can change. Why: libadb accepts any server, so an app holding a port adbd left could otherwise receive typed text and pastes. Cost: one receiver and about 100 ms to 1 s per connect (owner check (a)14).
- **R59 (final review): the phone's start deadline is 18 s (the Mac's stays 20 s), and the phone reports its end reason before it closes the Mac's links and runs `pkill`,** so the Mac shows the phone's real reason, not "The connection to the phone dropped." or "The phone didn't respond."

### Other behaviour for the results (from the reviews)

- **Auto-quality** steps down at 6 to 7 seconds with at most about 1.6 MB queued (in the Task 9 tests with a simulated link), and keeps its lateness history across encoder restarts. A preset picked by hand now resets the step-up back-off (final review M9).
- **The relay limit follows the bit rate (ruling R57, final review wave).** The video backlog that ends a session with LINK_SLOW is about 3 seconds of video: `video_bit_rate / 8 × 3` bytes, kept between 512 KiB and 4 MiB (750 kB at Low, 1.5 MB at Balanced, 3 MB at High). The first limit, a flat 4 MiB, let the picture trail some 17 seconds at Low before the session ended. Auto still steps down first: it acts with at most about 1.6 MB queued at High.
- **Parked review notes for a final pass:** a decoder restart race (a deferred reset can send one extra RESET_VIDEO), the last audio-engine release can land on a notification thread, a failed audio restart marks the session's audio broken until it ends, a cancelled `open()` thread can linger on the phone, and the marker check samples 8 patches (accepted risk).
- **No Opus-to-AAC fallback.** A phone with no Opus encoder sends codec 1, and the helper exits (upstream behaviour). Android 11 and later ship one.
- **The helper jar is pushed for each session** (about 0.4 s), to its own path (`anmirror-helper-<scid>.jar`; jars never launched are swept after a minute), and the toolchain-dependent jar hash is an accepted follow-up. The push is closed with the session's other ADB streams, so adbd going silent mid-push can't keep a session (and the ADB link) open.
- **Interop covers the relay, not the phone.** The simulated phone replays a recorded sample; the real helper, ADB and input paths are not exercised without hardware.

## Known limitations in 0.5

- **Legacy `adb tcpip` relay (residual of R58).** The peer proof stops an app on the phone from posing as Wireless debugging. It does not cover one rarer case: if you have also turned on the older network ADB (`adb tcpip`) and accepted this app's key there, an app holding Wireless debugging's old port could relay the login to that older ADB, pass the proof, and read what you type from the Mac. Leave the older network ADB off. A later release can refuse connections that did not use TLS.

- **The phone's clipboard reaches the Mac automatically only while the phone screen is shown** (header deviation 1). Outside a session, use the tile, the notification action or the Share sheet.
- **Full mode needs the phone on a Wi-Fi network.** Wireless debugging is off on mobile data and on the phone's own hotspot.
- **After a reboot or a network change, Full mode needs "Re-enable Full mode".** Android turns Wireless debugging off; the app cannot turn it back on.
- **Some phones need a maker toggle for taps and typing** ("USB debugging (Security settings)" on Xiaomi, Redmi and POCO; "Disable permission monitoring" on OPPO, OnePlus, realme, vivo and iQOO).
- **Apps that block screenshots show black** (banking apps and others).
- **Audio plays on the Mac, not on the phone, while mirroring with audio on.** Turn "Play phone audio on this Mac" off to keep sound on the phone. Android 11 may need the phone unlocked when the session starts.
- **Typing emoji or non-Latin text replaces the phone's clipboard,** because it goes through paste.
- **No pinch or multi-finger gestures, and no hover.** The Mac sends one finger.
- **The delay figure is a round trip, measured to decode, not to the display.** It does not include the Mac's display or the phone's own screen delay; the 240 fps film in check (b) is the way to see the whole path.
- **The helper jar is pushed for each session (about 0.4 s), and an Auto quality change shows a gap of about 1.5 s** while the encoder restarts.
- **`spake2-android` is LGPL-3.0 with prebuilt libraries, and libadb comes from JitPack:** F-Droid needs a from-source build (a recorded follow-up).
- **The branch as a whole carries one new Android permission, `REQUEST_IGNORE_BATTERY_OPTIMIZATIONS`, from the keep-alive patch,** which Google Play restricts. A Play flavour would have to drop it or justify it.
- **VoiceOver cannot read the mirrored screen** (PRD §8, out of scope).
- **Not exercised without hardware: everything in Part 2.** The helper launch, the ADB pairing and connection, the abstract-socket opens, the relay on a real link, real VideoToolbox and audio output, input methods, the clipboard, the screen-off paths and the battery are all untested on a real phone. The unit tests and interop cover the logic through fakes and a simulated phone only.
