Your Android phone, part of your Mac.

Mac Phone Bridge is a pair of native apps, one for your Mac and one for your phone. Notifications, calls, files, the clipboard and the phone's own screen come to your Mac, straight over your own network.

    Everything your phone does, on the Mac you're already using.

    Every feature below is built and in testing. None of them needs developer settings on the phone, except Full mode.

    On your Mac, as it happens

    Notifications with actions
    Phone notifications show up as real Mac notifications, with their buttons and inline reply. Pick which apps come through. Dismiss one on either device and it's gone on both.
    One-time codes
    When a notification holds a sign-in code, the Mac offers Copy code. It reads the notification text only, never your SMS.
    Calls
    See who's calling, then answer, decline, mute or hang up from the Mac. The call's audio stays on your phone.
    Caller ID
    Your contacts sync to an encrypted store inside the Mac app, used for caller names. One switch turns it off and wipes it.

    Moving things across

    Shared clipboard
    Copy text on the Mac and paste it on the phone. From the phone, send it with a Quick Settings tile or the share sheet, or automatically in Full mode. Text a password manager marks as secret is not sent.
    Files both ways
    Drag and drop onto the Phone window, use the Mac's Share menu, or Send to Mac from the phone's share sheet. Browse, download and upload the phone's files from the Mac.
    Instant screenshots
    Take a screenshot on the phone and it lands on the Mac, on the Desktop, in Downloads or on the clipboard.
    Recent photos
    The newest photos and videos as thumbnails on the Mac. Drag them out into any app.

    Control and context

    Music controller
    Track, artist and artwork from the phone, with play, pause, skip and volume on the Mac.
    Do Not Disturb and Focus
    A Focus on the Mac turns on Do Not Disturb on the phone, and the other way round with two Shortcuts you make once.
    Find my phone
    Ring it at full volume from the Mac, even when it's on silent.
    Phone status in the menu bar
    Battery and charging, signal, Wi-Fi or mobile data, and whether it's connected.
    Auto-lock when you walk away
    Opt-in. The Mac locks when your phone's Bluetooth signal stays away. It never unlocks the Mac.
    Trackpad and presentation remote
    Move the Mac's pointer, click and scroll from the phone, or change slides with a laser dot.

    Full mode, Android 11 and later

    The whole phone, in a Mac window.

    Full mode shows the phone's screen in a resizable Mac window, with its sound, and hands control to your Mac. Click to tap, scroll to scroll, drag to swipe. Type into WhatsApp or any other app with the Mac's keyboard, emoji and other languages included.

    • Back, Home, Recents, Rotate and volume in the window's toolbar
    • Turn the phone's screen off while you work from the Mac
    • Drop files on the window to send them to the phone
    • The phone's clipboard reaches the Mac automatically

    One-time setup

    Full mode needs Developer options and Wireless debugging on the phone, once. The phone app walks you through it. Turn Wireless debugging off and Full mode is off. Apps that block screenshots, such as some banking and video apps, show black.

    Phone to Mac, and nothing in between.

    The two apps find each other on your local network and talk directly. There is no server, so there is nothing to sign in to.

    1. Pair once

      The Mac shows a QR code. Scan it with the phone, then confirm that the same 6-digit code shows on both screens.

    2. Connect directly

      From then on they find each other on the same network and connect with mutual TLS 1.3, using certificates pinned at pairing. An unpaired device can't connect.

    3. Stay on your devices

      Nothing is relayed through a server. Remove the pairing from either side whenever you like.

    Private by design

    • No account. Nothing to sign up for, nothing to sign in to.
    • No cloud. Your data stays on your two devices.
    • No telemetry. No analytics. If crash reporting is ever added, it will be opt-in.
    • Never reads SMS. The Android app asks for no SMS permission.
    • Encrypted at rest. Synced contacts are stored encrypted on the Mac, and wiped when you turn caller ID off or unpair.
    • Open source. Every line is on GitHub under Apache-2.0, for anyone to read.

    What you need.

    One Mac and one Android phone on the same network. A 5 GHz Wi-Fi network gives Full mode its best picture.

    There's no download yet. To try it today, build both apps on a Mac.

    Terminal, on your Mac
    git clone https://github.com/venkataPhanindraVutla/mac-phone-bridge.git
cd mac-phone-bridge
# then follow "Build it" in README.md

    You'll need Xcode, xcodegen, protoc, JDK 21 and the Android SDK. The README's Build it section has the exact versions and commands.

    Questions.

    Something missing? Ask on GitHub issues.

    Is it free?

    Yes. It's free and open source under the Apache-2.0 licence. There's nothing to buy and no paid tier.

    Do I need an account?

    No. There is no account and no server. You pair your phone and your Mac once with a QR code, and they talk to each other directly.

    Which phones does it work with?

    Android 10 or later for everything except Full mode, which needs Android 11 or later. The beta has testers on several phone models, and the test plan covers Pixel, Samsung, Xiaomi, OnePlus and Motorola phones. Some makers add an extra developer switch for Full mode; the phone app tells you when yours does.

    Does it read my messages?

    It never asks for SMS permission, so it can't read your text messages. It shows the notifications you allow, from the apps you choose, and one-time codes are found in that notification text only.

    Why isn't it on the Play Store or the App Store yet?

    It isn't released yet. The plan for 1.0 is GitHub Releases for both apps and F-Droid for the Android app, with Google Play later. The Mac app won't be on the Mac App Store: controlling the pointer and keyboard doesn't fit the App Store's sandbox, so it will be downloaded directly.

    Is it safe?

    Pairing uses a QR code and a 6-digit code you confirm on both devices. After that, every connection is mutual TLS 1.3 with certificates pinned at pairing, so an unpaired device can't connect. Nothing leaves your local network. Full mode's helper runs only while the Mac shows the phone's screen and accepts commands only from your paired Mac. The code is open for anyone to review.

    Does it work when my phone and Mac are on different networks?

    Not in version 1. Both need to be on the same local network.

    Are the screens on this page real?

    They're drawn for this page from the apps' real layouts and wording, with made-up names and messages.
